Now · 2024 to present

Clinical text as a data product

I lead the clinical notes program at TriNetX, a global real-world data network: the NLP that turns physician narrative into structured, queryable facts for life-sciences research. I run it end to end, from pricing and governance to the prototypes that set the engineering roadmap.

Product

  • Pilot to launched product. Defined the offerings, took pricing through sign-off, and wrote the launch brief for general availability.
  • Launched multimodal notes search, clinical text beside structured data in the query builder, and a workspace for reading notes on the platform.
  • Set the program's objectives and turned the monthly reporting slide into a live scorecard.
  • Own the data-governance case for commercializing extracted facts and the reference document behind every customer-facing claim.

Scale and quality

  • Multiplied the footprint of contributing organizations and extracted facts, across three countries, in under a year.
  • Doubled the finished NLP pipelines across oncology biomarkers, performance status, smoking, cardiology, and pulmonary modules, each with measured precision.
  • Built the measurement layer: an accuracy inventory with reconciliation checks, a seller-facing data explorer, and the first extracted-versus-loaded gap audit.
  • Cleared a large medication-to-RxNorm mapping backlog without a language model, by reframing the problem to its distinct generic strings.

Prototypes that shipped

  • Clinical note-search copilot on an LLM with Elasticsearch, demoed live at AMIA 2026; now the spec for the production port.
  • An assertion-extraction harness on an open-weight model that showed how much a cohort changes when negated mentions are removed.
  • A multi-agent RFI responder, a program assistant in Slack, a media-monitoring agent over trial registries and FDA feeds, and a de-identification design for the whole corpus.
  • Each one handed to engineering with a spec and tests.

Path · 2000 to 2024

Bedside to data product

Every job on this line is about the same problem from a different side: how clinical information gets recorded, normalized, and trusted. The pharmacy years are why the informatics years work.

  1. 2000 – 2011

    Sterile Products PharmacistMassachusetts General Hospital

    Quality control and compatibility for IV and parenteral nutrition admixtures. Eleven years of seeing what actually gets written in a chart.

  2. 2003 – 2006

    Team Lead, Quality ManagementBoston Medical Center

    Managed 35 direct reports; built the USP 797 compounding program and an admixture manufacturing line that paid for itself many times over.

  3. 2007 – 2011

    Pharmacist Knowledge EngineerPartners HealthCare

    Curated the drug-drug interaction content used by every Partners clinical application. Co-authored the JAMIA paper on high-priority interactions for EHRs.

  4. 2011 – 2013

    Senior Business AnalystPartners HealthCare

    Specifications for the enterprise longitudinal medication record and the analysis behind Meaningful Use certification.

  5. 2013 – 2014

    CrossFit and Weightlifting CoachFull time, then on the side through 2024

    Two years out of informatics, coaching classes and teaching Olympic weightlifting clinics around New England. Changed more lives per hour than any job before or since.

    Cerner support, standardized pharmacy terminology, compounding software that removed TPN transcription errors, NICU order entry.
